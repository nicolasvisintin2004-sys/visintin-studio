You asked for three quotes for the same thing and got back $200,000, $800,000 and $2,500,000. The logical reaction is to assume two of the three are overcharging. It almost never is: all three are quoting different things and none of them explained that to you.

This note is the explanation you didn't get.

## Why quotes vary so much

A website isn't a product with a fixed size. When someone hands you a number, there are three decisions inside it that you can't see.

**Who builds it.** A template put together in an afternoon doesn't cost the same as a site written for your business. Both are called "a website".

**What it includes.** One quote covers design and development. Another adds the copy, the photos, loading the content and training you to use it. The gap between those two quotes can be double, and both are honest.

**What it's built on.** A site made in a visual builder costs less and ships sooner. It also loads slower, is harder to rank, and ties you to a monthly subscription that goes up every year. A site built with standard code costs more upfront and doesn't lock you into anyone.

Before comparing three numbers, ask all three to write down what's included. That accounts for most of the difference.

## The 2026 market ranges

Reference figures for the Argentine market, for an experienced independent developer:

| Type of site | Range |
| --- | --- |
| One-page landing | $300,000 – $700,000 |
| 5 to 8 page company site | $600,000 – $1,500,000 |
| Online store | Starts above the company site |

An agency quotes between 40% and 80% more for the same work. That's not a rip-off: you're paying for structure, a team and backup. If your business needs that, it makes sense. If you're a workshop of eight people, probably not.

One detail that confuses everyone: **most of the industry quotes in US dollars**. It isn't posturing. Hosting, domains, image services and tooling are all paid in foreign currency. Anyone quoting you in pesos twelve months out is either padding the number to cover themselves or is going to lose money and let you know halfway through the project.

## What any serious quote should include

Without these, it isn't a finished site:

- A domain registered in your name
- Hosting and an SSL certificate
- Responsive design that works on a phone before a desktop
- Technical SEO: titles, descriptions, sitemap, speed
- A contact form or button that reaches somewhere you actually read
- Training so you know what you have

And these are almost never included, though everyone assumes they are:

- **The copy.** Writing what each page says is separate work. If it wasn't quoted, you're going to write it.
- **The photos.** Nobody is going out to shoot them for the price of the site. Yours from a phone are more useful than you'd think, but someone has to select and prepare them.
- **Maintenance.** See the next section.

## The cost nobody mentions

A website isn't a finished building: it's something that has to be kept running.

Without maintenance, one of three things happens within a year: it breaks with an update, it goes stale with prices and models you no longer sell, or it gets slow and Google starts showing it less. None of them announce themselves. One day you find out because a customer tells you they couldn't find you.

The real 2026 range runs from $25,000 to $150,000 per month depending on the level of service. The acceptable minimum covers hosting, domain, SSL, backups and security updates. A good one also includes a few monthly hours to change content, because prices and models change.

If a quote doesn't mention maintenance, it isn't free: you just haven't been told about it yet.

## The three warning signs

**"Your website in 72 hours."** It's a template with your logo on top. In 72 hours there is no physical time to understand a business. You'll end up with a site that could belong to anyone, because it literally does.

**They don't put the domain in your name.** It's the most serious and the most common. If the domain is registered to whoever built your site, you don't have a supplier: you have a hostage situation, and you're the hostage. Ask for the domain in your name from day one, and for the access credentials.

**They won't show you the Lighthouse score of previous work.** Lighthouse is Google's speed measurement, it's free, and anyone can run it in two minutes. If they tell you they build fast sites and can't show you the number for something they already delivered, chances are they never measured it.

## How to decide how much to invest

The framing error is thinking of it as an expense. Expenses get minimised. This one doesn't.

Put concrete numbers on the table. If you sell a $500,000 product and the site brings you two sales a year you weren't going to get, it's already paid for itself with room to spare. If you sell USD 50,000 units, **one single** enquiry that converts makes the most expensive site on the market look cheap.

The right question isn't "how much does it cost". It's: **how many new customers do I need for it to pay for itself**. When the answer is "one" or "two", the decision stops being about price and becomes about trusting whoever is going to build it.

And if the answer comes out at twenty, then no: that quote isn't for your business.

## To close

Ask them to write down what's included. Ask for the domain in your name. Ask to see the Lighthouse score of something they've already delivered.

Those three questions will sort out any comparison of quotes, whoever you're asking.
